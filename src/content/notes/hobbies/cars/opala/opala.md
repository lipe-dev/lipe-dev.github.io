# Chevrolet Opala Diplomata (1988)

Meet Apagão.

The name came from my daughter. She heard me talking about the "Opalão" and understood "Apagão" (blackout, in Portuguese). Considering this car died 1km after I bought it, she nailed it.

## Why an Opala

This one runs in the family. My dad had an Opala coupe, my uncle had a 4 door Diplomata just like this one. I always liked them, and the Opala is a Brazilian GM icon: big, rear wheel drive, and with that glorious straight six.

Then a good price one showed up. It needs LOTS of work, which honestly makes it even better.

## The Car

- **Model:** 1988 Opala Diplomata, 4 door
- **Engine:** 250S (4.1 straight six)
- **Color:** two tone gray, the classic "blusa e saia" combo (lighter on top, darker on the bottom)
- **Condition on arrival:** rough but running. Bad respray at some point, cracks in the paint, no major rust in sight. Mechanically bad. The interior is just plain bad: torn seats, cracked dash and trim, sagging headliner, missing parts.

### The Swap Mystery

It left the factory with the 2.5 four cylinder. Now it has a 250S six. Either someone swapped the engine, or someone already did some tinkering with the transmission. The shifter gave it away (more on that below).

## Bringing It Home

I bought it on August 5, 2026. The exact day my son was born.

I was in the hospital with my wife. Meanwhile, my brother was two towns over negotiating with the seller. Priorities were very clear, and somehow both things got done.

Four days later, on August 9 (Father's Day, a Sunday), my dad and brother went there to drive it back. I stayed home taking care of things, just back from the hospital with a newborn.

The car started. Only first and third gears worked. It made it about 1km and died.

Meanwhile, we went to my grandma's for lunch, and I decided to go check on them. Glad I did: they were stuck on the side of the road waiting for the tow truck. Apagão got towed the remaining 40km.

Then, on our way back, my dad's Fiesta overheated. He hopped over a farm fence to get some water from a cow stable, which got us to the next town, where it overheated again. So I drove us all home.

Later that night we went back for the Fiesta (with a Burger King stop on the way, obviously). My dad had a theory: "if it cools down enough, the water will get to where it needs when we fill it back up, and we can drive home." He was right. It made it home.

Honestly, a fun Father's Day overall. A proud start for Apagão.

## What We Fixed So Far

### Carburetor

It wasn't running smoothly, so we rebuilt the carb. Now it purrs like a cat.

### Hoses

Some hoses started spilling water, so those got replaced.

### Throttle Cable

The gas pedal started to feel sloppy. New cable, problem solved.

### The Shifter (and the Swap Evidence)

The gearbox itself was fine. The shifter wasn't, for two reasons.

First, someone had replaced it with a weirdly fitting aftermarket one that didn't engage all gears properly. We took the good parts from it, put them in the old shifter, and all gears started engaging.

Second, and this is what makes me think a swap happened: the four cylinder engine is shorter than the six. If you swap in a six and keep the shifter in the same spot on the transmission main rod, it ends up sitting 7cm further back than it should. The proper adaptation is drilling a new hole 7cm forward, cutting some cast material from the transmission casing, and reinstalling the shifter there.

We did all that, and then replaced it with a chrome Iron Fort shifter. Looks sick as fuck.

### Fuel Tank and Lines

It was smoking a lot, and we got some intel that there was diesel in the tank. I still believe that's bullshit, but I replaced the tank and lines anyway. It smokes less now, but still a lot.

Regardless, everything runs somewhat smoothly now.
